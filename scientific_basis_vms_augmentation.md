# Scientific and Machine Learning Basis for VMS Deposit Label Augmentation (45 to 250)

## 1. Context: Dataset Structure & Class Imbalance

* **Positive Class ($n = 45$):** The 45 officially recognized VMS deposits of the Bathurst Mining Camp (BMC), curated from Geological Survey of Canada (GSC) Bulletin 566 (van Staal et al., 2003) and GeoNB records.
* **Negative Class ($n = 250$):** A hybrid negative dataset consisting of:
  1. **125 confirmed barren drill intercepts** (from New Brunswick Geological Survey collar records).
  2. **125 feature-space dissimilar pseudo-absences** (selected by maximizing Mahalanobis distance from the positive centroid across multi-dimensional feature space).
* **Initial Class Imbalance Ratio:** $\approx \mathbf{1:5.55}$ ($45 \text{ VMS} \text{ vs. } 250 \text{ barren}$).

---

## 2. Machine Learning Rationale

### A. Mitigating Majority Class Bias
Machine learning classifiers (such as Random Forest and XGBoost) optimize global loss functions across all training observations. Under an imbalanced ratio ($1:5.55$), standard loss functions penalize majority-class errors far more heavily than minority-class errors. An unaugmented classifier achieves **~85% overall accuracy** simply by predicting "barren" everywhere—resulting in near-zero sensitivity (recall) for rare VMS mineralization. Mineral prospectivity mapping specifically requires high sensitivity to identify rare target zones.

### B. Impact of Spatial Block Cross-Validation
To prevent spatial autocorrelation bias, a 5-fold Spatial Block Cross-Validation scheme is implemented (Brenning, 2012). Holding out an entire geographic block leaves only **~30 to 35 positive deposit instances per fold** during training. Augmenting the minority class inside training folds ensures consistent gradient calculations, tree-split selections, and loss function stability across spatial folds.

---

## 3. Augmentation Method: SMOTE Mechanics

Instead of naive random oversampling (which duplicates identical point observations and causes decision trees to memorize exact coordinates), the pipeline uses the **Synthetic Minority Over-sampling Technique (SMOTE; Chawla et al., 2002)**:

1. **Feature-Space Interpolation:** SMOTE selects a minority instance $x_i$ and one of its $k$-nearest minority neighbors $x_{\text{neighbor}}$ in multi-dimensional feature space (geophysical derivatives + till geochemistry CLR-PCA/MEAS scores).
2. **Synthetic Generation:** A synthetic vector $x_{\text{new}}$ is generated via linear interpolation:
   $$x_{\text{new}} = x_i + \lambda (x_{\text{neighbor}} - x_i), \quad \lambda \sim U(0, 1)$$
3. **Class Parity ($1:1$ Ratio):** Oversampling brings the positive count from 45 to **250 synthetic positive instances**, yielding a balanced dataset ($n = 250$ positive, $n = 250$ negative, total $N = 500$).

---

## 4. Geoscientific Validity of Feature-Space Interpolation

Interpolation between known deposit vectors is scientifically valid for VMS prospectivity mapping due to the physical nature of ore-forming systems:

1. **Continuous Hydrothermal Footprints:** VMS deposits are not isolated mathematical singularities; they are generated by hydrothermal fluid circulation systems that leave continuous physical and chemical gradients in host rocks and overlying till (e.g., alteration haloes, Zn-Pb-Cu-Ag-As-Sb-Cd pathfinder plumes, radiometric Th/K ratios, and gravity/magnetic anomalies).
2. **Plausible Synthetic Signatures:** Interpolation between two deposits in feature space creates synthetic samples representing plausible intermediate positions within this continuous prospectivity manifold (e.g., a deposit signature with slightly altered element ratios or derivative intensities lying between two real VMS systems of similar geological style).
3. **Generalization Over Memorization:** It expands the positive class boundary so decision trees learn generalized multivariate rules (e.g., high Th/K alteration + strong gravity horizontal gradient + elevated pathfinder score) rather than memorizing the exact values of 45 specific locations.

---

## 5. Methodological Safeguards Against Leakage

* **Strict In-Fold Application:** In the dataset building scripts (`build_dataset.py`) and methodological documentations, SMOTE is applied **exclusively within cross-validation training folds**. Validation folds remain unaugmented and untouched.
* **No Validation Contamination:** Synthetic samples never leak into held-out validation blocks, ensuring reported evaluation metrics reflect true model generalization to untested ground.

## 6. Empirical Validation Results

The empirical results in the project manuscript (`manuscript_NRR.md`) confirm that SMOTE augmentation successfully stabilized training without introducing over-prediction bias:

| Metric | Random Forest (RF) | XGBoost | Scientific Interpretation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **ROC-AUC** | **$0.927 \pm 0.047$** | $0.915 \pm 0.056$ | High overall class separation across operating thresholds |
| **Balanced Accuracy** | **$0.835 \pm 0.052$** | $0.844 \pm 0.077$ | Confirms no systematic bias toward over-predicting positive labels |
| **Success Rate AUC** | **$0.968$** | $0.940$ | Exceptional targeting efficiency across ranked camp area |
| **Deposit Capture (Top 10%)** | **$91.1\%$** | $86.7\%$ | Captures 41/45 deposits within top 10% prospective ground |
| **Deposit Capture (Top 30%)** | **$100.0\%$** | $97.8\%$ | Captures 45/45 deposits within top 30% prospective ground |
